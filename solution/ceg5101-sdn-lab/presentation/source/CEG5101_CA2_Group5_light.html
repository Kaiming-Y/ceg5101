The design reason behind each detection rule is a concrete failure. Count all ICMP, and the victim's echo replies get h6 blocked, with our flow. So we skip echo replies.

[click] The reference detector updates before it compares: the real threshold becomes about 21 times the baseline, and the attack poisons that baseline. We compare first and never learn from an anomaly.

[click] A fixed 3x ignores how bursty a source is, so our threshold is the largest of 3b, b plus 4 sigma, and 500, after a warm-up. A block is a priority-5000 drop rule on all six switches.

[Speaker B · Danze] target 1:10 · slot 3:15–4:30

Thanks, Kaiming. Dijkstra only understands numbers, so we put the verdict into the link cost, back to s2–s6. Cost is C0, plus load weight alpha 10 times utilisation, plus anomaly weight beta 30 times the flag A. The load term reacts fast, but after the block it fades within a few polls. Beta times A is the memory: A stays 1 while the drop rule keeps counting, then 10 seconds more.

[click] On s2–s6: 1.19 before the flood, 3.67 at the block, then a plateau at 3.14.

[click] Beta must beat the 3-longer detour, our own load of about 2.7, and margin 2: about 7.7. Thirty leaves room.

[click] Testable prediction: each attacked link plateaus at C0 plus 30, over C0, the table on the right. We check it on slides 10 and 11.

A price alone moves nothing. When should a flow actually move?

Why hold A (D7): the flood is dropped at the ingress, so R falls to 0 within a few polls. A stays 1 while the drop rate is at least 0.5T, then 10 s more. Why 0.5T (D8): the attacker's normal 1 pps ping also hits the drop rule. At the moment of the block the leftover flood load helps: R1 needed only β > 3.2, R3 > 6.9. From static costs alone: 22 − 19 + 2 = 5. Rmax = 40 Mbps; C0 rewritten for the 11 links (D1). s2-s6 returns to ×1.0 at 151.6 s, when h1 is still on the detour.

Every poll, each flow compares its path with the cheapest alternative. First, a what-if: we add the flow's own load to candidate links it does not use yet; otherwise h1's own 10 megabits make its current path look worse, and it flaps.

[click] Second, move only if at least 2 cheaper: that margin is our hysteresis.

[click] Third, egress first: s6, then s3, then the branch point s2 switches over; s1 is unchanged. No packet meets a missing rule.

[click] Case b in R1, the bars: stay 58.8, move 30.0, so it leaves; after the hold, 24.5 beats 30.1, so it returns. Link failures use the same rule.

Details: when leaving, s6 keeps its rule, s3 gets a new rule to s6, s2 is rewritten from →s6 to →s3, s1 is rewritten in place (next hop still s2). Nothing is deleted when leaving; the stale rule on s3 is deleted on the return.
